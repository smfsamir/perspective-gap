i# Defining Perspective

**Perspective** is the stance of a specific actor as represented in text, realised through attribution that links content to a source. It may involve a statement, belief, perception, desire, judgement, decision, intention, or emotion, and can appear as a quotation, indirect speech, or paraphrase of mental states. Neutral narration without attribution is excluded.

> **Note:** See Wiebe's [*Tracking Point of View in Narrative*](https://dl.acm.org/doi/pdf/10.5555/972525.972529), which treats perspective as psychological POV and distinguishes subjective sentences from objective narration.

**Actors** can be individuals or institutions, including metonymous references where objects represent institutions once the referent is clear.

Perspective is not analysed atomically at the level of single sentences but **contextually across stretches of text**, since journalists often drop explicit attribution after the first cue; a stance may only be recognised in relation to preceding or surrounding paragraphs.

## Attributed non-knowledge or non-disclosure

Expressions of non-knowledge and non-disclosure are analysed as perspectives because they attribute a stance to a source, even when no factual information about the event is conveyed. For example:

- *"Police could not say whether the suspect shot at police…"* (Article 1, Paragraph 12) expresses an **epistemic** stance of uncertainty.
- *"Skinner declined to comment on the shooting…"* (Article 6, Paragraph 23) expresses a **volitional** stance of refusal.

Both cases signal the actor's orientation toward participation in discourse and function as private-state reports anchored to an experiencer, rather than as neutral narration.

## Institutional decision or judgement

Institutional decisions and judgements are analysed as perspectives when they attribute a stance to an organisation.

In *"The civilian police watchdog, which is now investigating the series of shootings, has designated that officer and his actions as the subject of its probe"* (Article 6, Paragraph 6), the verb "has designated" conveys an institutional decision that defines the scope of the investigation. This is a source-linked stance rather than neutral narration. The reference to "the civilian police watchdog" functions metonymically for the Special Investigations Unit (SIU) once the referent is established.

By contrast, *"Postmedia has learned…"* attributes knowledge to the news outlet itself, which remains narration unless journalistic actors are explicitly treated as sources in the analysis.

## Contextual continuation of perspective

Perspective may continue across sentences even without repeated attribution.

In Paragraph 22 of Article 2, *"It's another deadly police shooting that highlights why Old Brewery Mission CEO Matthew Pearce believes police officers need training on how to deal with people in crisis"* anchors the stance to Pearce. The next sentence in Paragraph 23, *"The homeless shelter is working with the Montreal police force to do just that,"* carries forward the same perspective, despite lacking an explicit attribution marker.

The stance remains that of Pearce and the Old Brewery Mission until another source is introduced or narration returns to neutral.
